Inside the experiment

Physics

The slice is a tetrahedral mesh of about 8,700 elements, simulated on the CPU with XPBD (extended position-based dynamics) in small fixed steps. Every edge is a soft spring, every tetrahedron keeps its volume, and the pale rind and skin are about 2.4× firmer than the flesh.

Your pointer pins a small patch of nodes, so the jelly stretches locally. Floor contact has Coulomb friction and a little restitution, and internal damping bleeds energy from edge vibrations so it wobbles, then settles.

Seeds and air bubbles are anchored with barycentric coordinates to the mesh, so they ride the deformation and rotate with the surface.

Rendering

Native WebGPU and WGSL. A back-face pass records the exit normal and distance, giving the thickness for Beer–Lambert absorption (ruby where it is thick, pale where it is thin). The studio is refracted through the slab with a touch of dispersion and cloudiness, then Fresnel reflections and glossy highlights are added.

The flesh, pale rind and skin each have their own material response. Soft floor shadows come from sphere proxies that follow the body, tinted by the light passing through it. The render is 4× MSAA.
